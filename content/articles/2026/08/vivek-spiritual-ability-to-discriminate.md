# *Vivek* - Spiritual ability to Discriminate

A year ago, my strategy for navigating life was straightforward:
- accept the default settings,
- question very little,
- let external circumstances dictate the direction.

If a tradition or a religious practice existed, I followed it simply because it
was present. If society provided a standard path regarding education, work and
social milestones, I complied without examining them. During that period, my
daily routine operated on autopilot. My mind remained passive and I accepted
that state of indifference.

At the time, that lack of engagement felt comfortable. However, looking back
over the past year, I now recognize that my attitude was merely ignorant. It was
simply a reluctance to engage - like eating sand because someone put it on a
plate; it fills the space, but it is not food.


## The Trap of Apathy
For a long time, I convinced myself that caring about nothing was a sensible
stance. But treating a human life as something to pass through without
deliberate intent means ignoring the actual mechanics of personal responsibility
and contribution.

Jain philosophy defines this condition as *Aviveka* - the fundamental lack of
discernment between what truly benefits the inner self and what constitutes
"nothing". My past indifference was a clear example of Aviveka. I did not care
about my spiritual roots, my cultural obligations or my traditional background
and I remained indifferent to that very neglect. It is simply a refusal to
participate meaningfully in life.


## The Software Update
Over the course of last year, my perspective has slightly shifted.

My view of the social order has changed. Now I believe *Viveka* - spiritual
discrimination - is necessary and I cannot and should not remain indifferent.

Developing *Viveka* does not mean I need to attempt to achieve something great
or release a new frontier AI model. It simply means recognizing the value of
conscious effort. It is about upgrading my mindset just enough to stop the habit
of not caring about what goes on around me.

The difference today is that I have accumulated some internal motivation to
understand my roots, my philosophy and my responsibilities, with a quiet desire
to live deliberately rather than simply letting time pass by default.
